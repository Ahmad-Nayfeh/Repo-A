## 3. Serious-game / engineering-education research

- [GD-40] A meta-analysis of the cognitive and motivational effects of serious games — Wouters, van Nimwegen, van Oostendorp, van der Spek, J. Educational Psychology 105(2):249–265, 2013 — https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-cognitive-and-motivational-effects-of-seri/ — verified (bibliographic only) / snippet-only (findings) — Learning d=0.29 vs conventional instruction (77 studies, N=5,547); retention d=0.36; not more motivating; larger gains when supplemented with other instruction, multiple sessions, group play; narrative not significant.
- [GD-41] A meta-analytic review of the role of instructional support in game-based learning — Wouters & van Oostendorp, Computers & Education 60(1):412–425, 2013, DOI 10.1016/j.compedu.2012.07.018 — snippet-only — Instructional support improves learning d=.34 (k=107); largest for skills (d=.62) and for support aiding selection of relevant info (d=.46).
- [GD-42] Digital games, design, and learning: a systematic review and meta-analysis — Clark, Tanner-Smith, Killingsworth, Review of Educational Research 86(1):79–122, 2016, DOI 10.3102/0034654315582065 — verified (SRI brief page: scope only) / snippet-only (numbers) — Games vs non-game g≈0.33 (k=57); augmented vs standard game designs ≈0.34 (k=20); moderators incl. visual realism; a summary says simpler visuals were as or more effective.
- [GD-43] A meta-analytic examination of the instructional effectiveness of computer-based simulation games — Traci Sitzmann, Personnel Psychology 64(2):489–528, 2011 — https://media.moresteam.com/main/downloads/sitzmann-games2010.pdf — snippet-only — 65 studies, 6,476 trainees: self-efficacy +20%, declarative +11%, procedural +14%, retention +9% vs comparison.
- [GD-44] PhET: Simulations That Enhance Learning — Wieman, Adams, Perkins, Science 322:682–683, 31 Oct 2008 — https://phet.colorado.edu/publications/PhET_Simulations_That_Enhance_Learning.pdf — verified (PDF text extracted) — Design features: familiar elements, show the invisible, multiple representations, direct manipulation, instruments, interview-tested graphics, "distortion and simplification of reality"; CCK 2-h exercise beat real-equipment lab on final-exam current/voltage; Radio Waves: simple start-up panel first, complex view later; students fixate on irrelevant details (wire colour) with real equipment.
- [GD-45] When learning about the real world is better done virtually — Finkelstein et al., Phys. Rev. ST Phys. Educ. Res., 2005 — https://www.compadre.org/portal/items/detail.cfm?ID=5272 — snippet-only — CCK substitution study; sim students built real circuits faster afterwards.
- [GD-46] Why minimal guidance during instruction does not work — Kirschner, Sweller, Clark, Educational Psychologist 41(2):75–86, 2006 — https://www.davidlewisphd.com/courses/EDD8121/readings/2006-Kirschner_et_al.pdf — snippet-only — Novices benefit from worked examples; expertise-reversal effect.
- [GD-47] Nine ways to reduce cognitive load in multimedia learning — Mayer & Moreno, Educational Psychologist 38(1):43–52, 2003 — https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/575/9_ways_to_reduce_CL.pdf — snippet-only — Segmenting, pretraining (names/characteristics of components), signalling, spatial contiguity, avoid redundancy.
- [GD-48] Fidelity in the design of instructional simulations — S. M. Alessi, J. Computer-Based Instruction 15(2):40–47, 1988 — https://learntechlib.org/p/170285 — snippet-only — Learning vs fidelity non-linear; optimal fidelity depends on learner stage (lower for novices).
- [GD-49] What Video Games Have to Teach Us About Learning and Literacy — James Paul Gee, Palgrave Macmillan, 2003 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Video_Games_Have_to_Teach_Us_About_Learning_and_Literacy — snippet-only — 36 learning principles (e.g., Active Critical Learning, Semiotic, Committed Learning, Identity, Insider); a reviewer calls them observations/opinion more than findings.

## 5. Arabic / RTL
